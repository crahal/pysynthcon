# Replication and validation

The four notebooks in `empirical_examples/` use empirical applications from four methodological studies. Each downloads immutable public bytes with requests,
verifies SHA256, checks a reference specification, and runs all twelve
interfaces on a shared outcome panel. Executed tables and embedded figures
are retained in the notebooks.

Each numerical check requires the complete historical calendar, the original
treatment boundary and a finite gap in every period. Each notebook also exports five publication figures (reference paths,
ATT/pretreatment-fit summary, shared-scale effect panels, pretreatment validation,
and donor-exclusion sensitivity) as PDF, SVG and
300 dpi PNG, with a CSV of comparison values. These exports and diagnostic
files are generated locally and excluded from version control; rerun the
notebooks to recreate them. The [plotting guide](plotting.md)
documents units, timing, scale and export conventions. Figures show point
estimates without confidence intervals; the post-period shading denotes time.

## Literature annotations

The twelve reference/comparison figures annotate source values and their
matched reproductions directly beside curves, effect panels or ATT rows.
Signed differences use the same statistic and evaluation window; these
descriptive comparisons are distinct from the numerical validation targets
below. Values retain the precision and interpretation of their sources:

| Setting | Figure annotation | Source and comparability |
|---|---|---|
| California | SDID mean effect −15.6 packs per capita, 1989–2000 | Arkhangelsky et al. (2021), [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.09970v4#page=8); no-covariate SDID. |
| Basque Country | Approximately 10% lower GDP per capita in the 1980s–1990s | Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003), [p. 117](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/The%20Economic%20Costs%20of%20Conflict.pdf#page=5). Figure 1 gives a visual headline. Our 1980–1997 mean follows the observed-GDP denominator in Figure 2. Both appear on the figure without a numerical difference. |
| Germany | Approximately −1,600 US$ per capita annually, 1990–2003 | Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller (2015), [p. 504](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Comparative%20Politics%20and%20the%20Synthetic%20Control.pdf#page=10). Historical covariate specification; the comparison fits differ. |
| Kansas | Ridge ASCM mean effect −0.0401 log points, 2012 Q2–2016 Q1 | Authors' [no-covariate vignette](https://github.com/ebenmichael/augsynth/blob/7e70072232fe75057aa8b5d7af08c7e1aef53ae8/vignettes/singlesynth-vignette.md#L217), pinned at commit `7e70072232fe75057aa8b5d7af08c7e1aef53ae8`. Labelled “Authors’ reference”, not a paper-printed headline. |

German GDP is in **PPP-adjusted current US dollars**, following the authors'
[August 2025 erratum](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2025-09/Comparative%20Politics%20and%20the%20Synthetic%20Control%20Method%20erratum.pdf).
The original paper's constant-2002-dollar label was incorrect. The correction
changes interpretation of levels, not the stored data or fitted estimates.

An ochre diamond marks a compatible source mean on its matched ATT row;
dotted segments mark compatible source means on effect panels over their
stated evaluation windows. Percentage comparisons on dollar axes and subset
comparisons on full-period ATT rows remain text only. The guides are neither
period-specific paths nor uncertainty intervals. An effect from one
specification is not an expected answer for every estimator.

Historical fits absent from the comparison mapping receive separate
`Reference · …` rows or panels. References are matched and deduplicated by
estimate-object identity, so similarly named methods do not inherit their
benchmarks. The plotted estimates and supplied mapping remain unchanged.
Short numbered citations identify the original sources; callouts link to them
in vector exports. Full reference
contexts and numerical checks remain in expandable notebook audit tables
and exported CSVs. The earlier source-strip layout remains available through
`reference_style="strip"`; see the [plotting guide](plotting.md).

## What each check establishes

| Example | Reference specification | Numeric target | Predeclared tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | R synthdid no-covariate example, 1989 onset | ATT −15.603827872733849 | absolute $10^{-6}$ |
| Basque | Authors’ Synth specification: 16 regions and four education shares | Independent R 1975–1997 ATT −0.6995627075801111; published Catalonia/Madrid weights 0.8508/0.1492 | absolute $10^{-4}$ for ATT, paths and weights |
| Germany | pysyncon ADH two-stage V specification | 1990–2003 ATT −1555.1346777620479 | 2.5% relative |
| Kansas | authors' R augsynth no-covariate vignette | Ridge ASCM ATT −0.0401 | absolute 0.00005 (half a unit at the printed precision) |

California's target was independently generated by running the pinned R
synthdid source and also matches the paper's rounded −15.6 result. The Basque target is an independent run of the authors’ original R Synth
algorithm (Synth 1.1-10) with the paper’s Table 3 predictor construction and
16-region donor pool. Its small numerical fixture records the package versions, predictor
values, weights and entire path. Germany’s high-precision number is an
**upstream implementation reference** using its stated parity tolerance.
Neither high-precision ATT is printed to that precision in the papers. Kansas checks the ATT at four decimal places and all sixteen post-treatment
quarterly effects at the three decimal places printed in the authors' vignette. Differences in third-decimal donor weights can arise from
nested optimization and numerical environments; tolerances are not widened
after seeing a run.

California's SDID standard error is also replicated with the authors' exact
200-draw plan. `california_paper_inference(panel, estimate)` uses an isolated
base-R process to generate Mersenne-Twister/Inversion/Rejection permutations
with R seed 12345, then performs all SDID refits in Python. The alphabetical
donor order and historical time window are checked. The standard error is
**8.36799308138054**, matching Table 1's rounded **8.4**, with an independent-R
absolute tolerance of `1e-9`. The seed is specified in the authors'
[paper-results vignette](https://github.com/synth-inference/synthdid/blob/70c1ce3eac58e28c30b67435ca377bb48baa9b8a/vignettes/paper-results.Rmd).
A NumPy seed produces a different draw plan, so it is unsuitable for claiming
replication of this particular finite-draw standard error. See the
[inference guide](inference.md) for the API and draw-level checks.

The Basque fitting period is 1960–1969; the common-comparison ATT begins 1970,
while the upstream reference ATT uses 1975–1997. All Basque fits exclude Spain’s aggregate, which contains the treated
region. The historical fit combines the two highest schooling categories and
averages annual education percentage shares over 1964–1969, matching
[paper Table 3](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/The%20Economic%20Costs%20of%20Conflict.pdf#page=4).
The later Synth manual normalizes mean headcounts instead, which gives slightly
different education predictors; the primary replication uses the paper’s table.
The earlier implementation incorrectly used the national aggregate and raw
education counts; matching its ATT did not establish specification parity. Germany's historical
training and predictor convention includes 1990, even though the comparison
Panel defines 1990 as post-treatment. The explicit `allow_post_predictors=True` exception is isolated in `replicate`, explained
in the notebook, and never used by the generic native estimators. Kansas uses
2012.25 as in the authors' example; the outcome is a natural logarithm.

The California dataset contains outcomes only. The notebook reproduces the
SDID specification, **not** ADH (2010)'s original covariate-based SCM predictor
specification. Native outcome SCM on that dataset is a separate comparison.

## Independent numerical checks

Offline fixtures in `tests/fixtures` contain small artificial numeric panels
and outputs generated by R, not by the Python estimators. SDID is checked
against R's donor weights, time weights and ATT. The current SCUL default is
checked against independently generated original-R fits with glmnet 4.0-2,
including all three selection rules. ISC is checked against MSCMT 1.4.0,
including covariate groups and the donor-sampling stream across individuals.
These checks live in `tests/test_r_references.py`; their generator is
`.local/reference/tests/reference_backends.R`.

Separate legacy tests check the explicitly selected native-Python SCUL
approximation against glmnet's solver on a common lambda grid. Lasso prediction
tolerances allow its stopping error; its alpha grid and optimal penalty are
checked separately. The analytic orthogonal-design lasso test also verifies
penalty normalization and reconstruction on original units.

Other tests cover known effects, multi-treated additive models, label/order
alignment, no treated-post leakage, invariance, nearest-donor selection,
case aggregation, placebo exclusion/rank rules, HTTP errors, hashes and
corrupt caches. Adapter tests compare returned predictions to the actual
upstream fitted object's predictions.

## Re-run

Complete notebook execution requires the separately supplied
[local reference bundle](reference_backends.md). Its R source and setup tools
are not distributed in this repository. Provision it before the commands below.

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements-replication.txt
python -m pip install -e '.[docs,dev]'
# First install R, C/Fortran development tools and GLPK headers (see guide below).
python .local/reference/scripts/install_r_references.py --library .r-library
python -m pytest
python -m pytest -m network
python scripts/execute_notebooks.py --output-dir /tmp/pysynth-notebooks
```

The tested numeric environment uses Python 3.12, NumPy 2.0.2, SciPy 1.13.1,
pandas 2.3.3 and scikit-learn 1.7.2; all principal replication requirements
are pinned. BLAS and platform differences can still matter. Run with
`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` for stable, economical small-panel computation.

R and the local bundle are required for the default SCUL and ISC fits and for
their integration tests. The native Python tests remain independent of R and
network access. Missing local resources produce explicit integration-test skips.
See the [R installation guide](reference_backends.md).

To independently re-run the legacy native-Python SCUL and SDID point-estimate
references without rewriting fixtures, install the R packages `glmnet` and
`jsonlite`, then run:

```bash
python .local/reference/scripts/validate_r.py
# For a custom R library location:
python .local/reference/scripts/validate_r.py --r-library /path/to/R-library
```

This legacy SCUL fixture tests `backend="python", selection="min"`; it does
not validate the current original-R default. It used R 4.6.2 and glmnet 5.2.
Its selected penalty
matches to $10^{-9}$. Default glmnet coefficient stopping leaves a maximum
path difference of about 0.007 outcome units on this smooth test panel;
the test permits 0.01 and also checks a high-precision glmnet refit at
$10^{-5}$ absolute path tolerance. These are solver parity checks, not a
precision claim about a causal effect in the original SCUL study.

## Boundaries of the empirical claims

Applying SCUL, ISC or 2SISC to a classic aggregate dataset is a new
application. It does not reproduce the original SCUL scanner-data study,
the Vagni–Breen motherhood study, or costofcare's survey analysis. The original
data access and case construction are not supplied by this repository.
`IndividualSC` uses each case's full eligible pool with MSCMT and 50 donor
resamples. Default `TwoStageIndividualSC` follows the care authors' raw
nearest-neighbor screen and temporal-mean predictor fit in pysyncon, with no
donor resampling. Both appear separately in every comparison notebook.
The earlier screened MSCMT variant remains explicitly available; it is not the
default two-stage pipeline. The original survey-specific variables,
eligibility rules and microdata are still required to reproduce those
studies' empirical estimates; no invented targets are assigned to them.


## Full fitting and local diagnostics

The empirical notebooks now estimate pysyncon SCM predictor importance, retain
all pre-outcome lags, and run all ten SyntheticControlMethods starts. SCUL uses
the original fold-specific glmnet paths and median penalty; ISC uses
MSCMT and 50 donor resamples. Default 2SISC uses the authors' nested
mean-predictor fit without donor resampling. Original SDID placebo inference
uses 200 refits.
Numerical solver recovery is documented in the backend guide; it changes no
statistical objective and records each optimizer attempt.

SCUL needs a feasible rolling-validation design. The explicit initial-window /
horizon choices are California 5/12, Germany 14/14, Kansas 16/16, and Basque
3/5. The Basque pre-period is too short to validate a 28-period horizon; this
limitation is visible rather than hidden by an automatic shortcut. These are
new applications of the original algorithm, not original-paper calibrations.

The notebooks retain the academic source comparisons in their tables and
figure annotations. Runtime, solver diagnostics, and SDID resampling records
are exported locally alongside the figures. No archived software-output files
are required to run the examples. Floating-point libraries and R package versions are recorded;
algorithmic parity does not imply bitwise equality across arbitrary machines.

The new evaluation sections compare eight candidates on common withheld
pretreatment blocks, including three 2SISC donor counts, then refit the
validation-selected candidate. A separate sensitivity exercise removes every
donor in turn for outcome SCM, 2SISC and SDID. These are new analyses, not
published numerical targets. See the [evaluation guide](evaluation.md) for
masking, complete-fit budgets, scoring and interpretation limits.

Basque, California and Germany each supply only two held-out observations
under the common folds allowed by these reference SCUL settings. These short
comparisons illustrate the workflow rather than establish a reliable ranking.
Some California/Germany SDID validation fits reach the original iteration cap;
the warnings and fit metadata remain visible, including when SDID is selected.

## SDID resample convergence checks

Each notebook retains its original-cap 200-draw inference result, then replays
the identical resampling plan with a 1,000,000-iteration final-stage budget and
`require_convergence=True`. No penalty, tolerance, warm start, sparsification
or resample is changed. The strict run must meet the original objective-decrease
rule for both weight fits in every draw; an unresolved fit stops execution.
The original-cap SDID point estimate remains fixed in this comparison.

The exported budget-comparison table shows both standard errors, their change,
the largest draw change and capped-draw counts. A separate CSV retains the
original and extended draw-level convergence diagnostics, including dual gaps.
This numerical extension is labelled separately from exact finite-iteration
replication. Original-cap warnings remain visible and are explained alongside
the comparison; the reference tolerances are unchanged.
